Deliberate Before You Fly: Vision-Guided Spatial Deliberation for UAV See-and-Reach Navigation

DBFly introduces vision-guided spatial deliberation for UAV see-and-reach navigation, explicitly modeling how to maneuver and when to terminate, thereby improving the consistency between high-level navigation intent and executable continuous flight control.

Abstract

A short summary of the task, challenge, and the proposed method.

UAV see-and-reach navigation requires an aerial agent to precisely approach and reliably stop near a language-specified target visible from the UAV's initial viewpoint. Existing methods often directly map vision-language understanding to action outputs, without explicitly modeling the fine-grained decision process needed for continuous 3D maneuvering and reliable termination.

We propose DBFly, a vision-language waypoint prediction framework based on vision-guided spatial deliberation. DBFly progressively performs target-direction anchoring, spatial diagnosis, and maneuver decision through a spatial maneuver decision chain, allowing continuous waypoint generation to be regulated by high-level navigation intent. Meanwhile, DBFly introduces a terminal-convergence-aware stopping strategy, which formulates navigation termination as a terminal event jointly supported by target proximity and short-horizon trajectory convergence.

Extensive experiments demonstrate that DBFly consistently outperforms strong baselines and shows promising performance in both simulation and real-world UAV experiments.

Core Ideas

  • Spatial Maneuver Decision Chain: explicitly models target-direction anchoring, spatial diagnosis, and maneuver decision before waypoint prediction.
  • Terminal-Convergence-Aware Stopping: improves stopping reliability by jointly considering target proximity and short-horizon trajectory convergence.
  • Vision-Language Backbone: a Qwen3-VL-based multimodal model produces structured navigation reasoning and executable control decisions.
  • Closed-Loop Navigation: the predicted high-level maneuver intent regulates continuous waypoint generation for more consistent and stable flight.

Key Advantages

  • Explicitly bridges high-level vision-language reasoning and low-level executable waypoint control.
  • Improves maneuver consistency through structured spatial deliberation.
  • Enhances termination reliability near the target with terminal-convergence-aware stopping.
